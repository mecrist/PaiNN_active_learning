================================================================================
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & ACTIVE LEARNING ESSENTIALS
================================================================================
In computational chemistry and materials science, training Machine Learning
Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs) by brute-force Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics (AIMD)
is computationally prohibitive and inherently redundant:
  - 95%+ of structures sampled during standard AIMD dwell in equilibrium basins.
  - Rare events, transition states, and high-energy configurations (e.g. bond
    breaking, water dissociation at mineral interfaces) are rarely sampled.
  - When the MLIP encounters these uncharted regions during MD, it extrapolates
    unphysically, causing simulation explosion or unphysical "flying atoms".

Active Learning (AL) solves this by closing the loop:
  1. An ensemble (committee) of MLIP models drives the MD simulation.
  2. The committee members predict forces independently; disagreement (variance
     or standard deviation) among them serves as an instantaneous proxy for
     model uncertainty.
  3. When the uncertainty exceeds a dynamic threshold, MD halts.
  4. The uncertain configuration is dispatched to a reference quantum chemistry
     code (here FHI-aims DFT).
  5. The DFT energy and forces are assimilated into the training set.
  6. The ensemble models are fine-tuned on the new data.
  7. The updated ensemble resumes MD exploration from where it left off.

This creates a self-healing, data-efficient MLIP that achieves chemical accuracy
with orders of magnitude fewer DFT evaluations.

2.3 HOW THE ACTIVE LEARNING STATE MACHINE WORKS (STEP-BY-STEP)
--------------------------------------------------------------
The active learning loop in `aims_PAX/procedures/active_learning.py` runs an
asynchronous event loop managing 10 concurrent trajectory workers:

Step 1: Initialization
  - Loads starting geometries and existing dataset (`train.xyz`, `valid.xyz`).
  - Initializes 3 MACE models with distinct random seeds (`seed: 100`, `123`, `777`).
  - Computes elemental reference atomic energies E0 from the training data:
      E0(H) = 1295.28 eV, E0(O) = -4671.17 eV, E0(Si) = -2659.40 eV.
  - Initializes Parsl WorkQueue executor in the background.

Step 2: Trajectory Propagation ("running" state)
  - MD trajectories (0 to 9) run on the GPU via ASE `Langevin` dynamics.
  - The mean committee force governs the atomic movement.
  - Trajectories save checkpoints periodically.

Step 3: Uncertainty Evaluation
  - Every 25 MD steps (`skip_step_mlff`), each model in the committee evaluates
    the structure:
      F_m(i) = force predicted by model m (m in {0, 1, 2}) on atom i.
  - The standard deviation of the atomic forces is computed:
      sigma_i = sqrt( (1 / (M - 1)) * sum_{m=1}^M || F_m(i) - <F(i)> ||^2 )
  - The global uncertainty is:
      U = max_i ( sigma_i )
  - U is compared against the adaptive threshold U_thresh.

Step 4: Out-of-Distribution Detection & Asynchronous DFT Query ("waiting" state)
  - If U > U_thresh (e.g. U = 65.8 meV/A vs threshold 14.0 meV/A):
      * Trajectory worker pauses and enters "waiting" state.
      * The structure is dispatched as an asynchronous Parsl task to Slurm.
      * Parsl submits a job to the CPU partition (`nanotubo`), invoking
        `mpirun aims.x` with `control.in`.
      * CRITICAL FEATURE: All other trajectories (whose uncertainty is below
        threshold) continue running MD on the GPU without waiting!

Step 5: Assimilation & Online Retraining ("training" state)
  - When FHI-aims finishes, Parsl returns energy, forces, and Hirshfeld charges.
  - If `valid_ratio` condition triggers, structure goes to validation; otherwise
    added to `train.xyz`.
  - Trajectory enters "training" state:
      * The committee models are fine-tuned on the updated dataset for
        `epochs_per_worker = 2` epochs on the GPU.
      * New weights are reloaded into the ASE calculator.
      * Trajectory state resets to "running" and resumes MD from the last step.

Step 6: Stopping Criteria & Convergence
  - Loop terminates when:
      a) All trajectories reach `max_MD_steps` (1000 steps), OR
      b) Total training size reaches `max_train_set_size` (200 structures), OR
      c) Validation error drops below `desired_acc`.
  - A full convergence training session is executed (`converge_al: true`),
    training the best model until no improvement for 50 epochs (`convergence_patience`).
  - Final models, datasets, and trajectories are saved to `results/`.


2.4 HPC ARCHITECTURE: PARSL, WORKQUEUE, AND SLURM SPLIT
-------------------------------------------------------
A key strength of aims-PAX is heterogeneous HPC task distribution:
  - Node A (GPU: `metano`): Runs `job_gpu.sh` (Python controller, ASE Langevin MD,
    MACE PyTorch forward/backward passes).
  - Nodes B..E (CPU: `nanotubo`): Parsl WorkQueue dynamically requests 32-core
    CPU nodes to run parallel MPI FHI-aims calculations.
This prevents expensive GPU nodes from sitting idle while DFT runs, and prevents
CPU nodes from being starved by sequential single-point calculations.

3. WHAT CAN BE DONE WITH THIS PIPELINE (USE CASES & SCIENTIFIC EXTENSIONS)
================================================================================

3.1 REACTIVE INTERFACE SAMPLING (SILICA / WATER DISSOCIATION)
------------------------------------------------------------
At mineral-water interfaces, water molecules adsorb, dissociate into OH- and H+,
protonate surface oxygens (silanols Si-OH), and attack Si-O-Si siloxane bridges.
Standard MD never sees bond cleavages because the activation barrier is 0.6-1.2 eV.
With this active learning pipeline:
  - Trajectories at 450K push molecules toward transition states.
  - As bonds stretch into the reaction barrier, committee models disagree sharply.
  - The transition state geometry is flagged (U >> U_thresh) and labeled by DFT.
  - The potential learns the exact reactive surface chemistry automatically!

3.2 HIGH-TEMPERATURE & PHASE TRANSITION EXPLORATION
---------------------------------------------------
By setting temperature ladders (e.g. 300K, 600K, 1000K, 1500K) or gradual heating,
the pipeline samples liquid silica, amorphous silica quenching, and coordination
defects (3-fold and 5-fold silicon atoms).

3.3 MULTI-POLYMORPH SIMULTANEOUS EXPLORATION
--------------------------------------------
Because `geometries/` accepts multiple input files, a single run simultaneously
samples:
  - Alpha-quartz bulk & surfaces
  - Beta-cristobalite bulk & surfaces
  - Amorphous silica slabs
  - Liquid water clusters and droplets
The resulting potential is globally transferable across all phases.

3.4 MINIMAL-BUDGET DATASET GENERATION
-------------------------------------
Instead of computing 20,000 AIMD steps (which wastes months of CPU hours on
repetitive vibrations), active learning selects only 100-300 maximally
informative structures, generating an ultra-compact, high-density training set.

3.5 RECALCULATING DATASETS WITH HIGHER DFT ACCURACY (aims-PAX-recalc)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Once the active learning loop collects 200 diverse structures with cheap PBE DFT,
the user can run:
  `aims-PAX-recalc --aimsPAX-settings recalc.yaml`
to re-evaluate all collected structures at hybrid-DFT (e.g. HSE06 or PBE0) or
with larger basis sets (e.g. `intermediate` or `tight`), upgrading model accuracy
at negligible cost.

3.6 UNCERTAINTY-DRIVEN DYNAMICS (UDD)
-------------------------------------
In `aims_PAX/tools/uncertainty.py`, `UDDCalculator` provides a bias potential
proportional to model uncertainty:
  V_bias(R) = + gamma * U(R)
This actively drives the MD trajectory toward regions of highest model ignorance,
acting as an autonomous transition-state finder.

4.3 CRITICAL DATA REQUIREMENT: ELEMENTAL REFERENCE ENERGIES (E0)
----------------------------------------------------------------
FHI-aims uses all-electron reference baselines, yielding absolute energies of
thousands of eV:
  - Hydrogen (Z=1):  +1295.16 eV / atom
  - Oxygen   (Z=8):  -4671.61 eV / atom
  - Silicon  (Z=14): -2659.60 eV / atom

PaiNN trains on referenced (zero-centered) energies:
  E_referenced = E_total - sum_i [ E0(Z_i) ]

If new structures from FHI-aims are added during active learning, their raw DFT
energies MUST be shifted by these exact same E0 reference values before training
PaiNN, otherwise loss explodes with NaN gradients!
These are stored in `reference_energies.json`.

5.1 STRATEGY COMPARISON
-----------------------
You have two architectural options to reproduce active learning with PaiNN:

Option A: Dedicated Python Active Learning Driver (RECOMMENDED)
  - Advantages:
      * Zero friction with Conda environments (`nff` vs `my_env`).
      * Direct use of `nff.io.ase_calcs.EnsembleNFF` or a custom PaiNN ASE calculator.
      * Exact control over E0 reference subtraction and NFF dataset caching.
      * Transparent, easy to debug, customize, and monitor.
  - How it works: A standalone Python script drives multiple ASE Langevin MD
    trajectories using the 3 PaiNN models, measures atomic force std-dev, submits
    FHI-aims single points to Slurm, appends new points to extxyz, and calls
    `finetune_painn.py` to update the models.

Option B: Extending aims-PAX to support PaiNN natively
  - Advantages: Uses the existing `aims-PAX-al` CLI interface.
  - Drawbacks: Requires modifying `aims_PAX` source code:
      * Adding `painn` to `aims_PAX/settings/model.py`.
      * Creating `aims_PAX/tools/model_tools/setup_painn.py`.
      * Writing a custom NFF training adapter in `aims_PAX/tools/model_tools/train_epoch.py`.
      * Resolving environment dependency conflicts (aims-PAX expects `e3nn` / `mace`,
        while PaiNN requires `nff` / PyTorch geometric stack).

We provide the complete implementation for Option A below (which is ready to run
immediately), followed by the technical blueprint for Option B.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python
"""Active Learning Driver for PaiNN Committee on Silica/Water Systems.

Closed-loop active learning using a 3-member fine-tuned PaiNN committee.
"""

import copy
import json
import os
import shutil
import subprocess
import time
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
import torch
from ase import Atoms, units
from ase.io import read, write
from ase.md.langevin import Langevin

from nff.data import Dataset, collate_dicts
from nff.data.graphs import get_neighbor_list
from nff.train.builders.model import load_model

# ==============================================================================
# 1. USER CONFIGURATION & PATHS
# ==============================================================================
WORK_DIR = Path("/home/maria.crist/pax/painn_al_run")
WORK_DIR.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)

# Path to the fine-tuned PaiNN committee from dft_mlip/my_dataset
PAINN_ENSEMBLE_DIR = Path(
    "/home/maria.crist/dft_mlip/my_dataset/mine/model_2_ep500/silica_Painn_model_finetuned/painn_ensemble"
)
REF_ENERGIES_PATH = Path(
    "/home/maria.crist/dft_mlip/my_dataset/mine/model_2_ep100/reference_energies.json"
)

# FHI-aims settings & binaries
CONTROL_IN = Path("/home/maria.crist/pax/control.in")
SPECIES_DIR = Path(
    "/home/maria.crist/fhi-aims.260331/species_defaults/defaults_2020/light"
)
AIMS_MODULE = "fhi-aims/260331"

# Active Learning Hyperparameters
NUM_TRAJECTORIES = 5
MAX_MD_STEPS = 1000
MD_TIMESTEP_FS = 0.5
SKIP_STEP_MLFF = 25
UNCERTAINTY_THRESHOLD_INIT = 20.0  # meV/A (max atomic force std dev)
MAX_NEW_POINTS = 50  # Maximum DFT single-point calculations
FINE_TUNE_EPOCHS = 10  # Epochs to retrain per newly added DFT point
DEVICE = "cuda:0" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu"
CUTOFF = 6.0


# ==============================================================================
# 2. PAINN COMMITTEE ASE CALCULATOR
# ==============================================================================
class PainnCommitteeCalculator:
    """ASE-compatible calculator for an ensemble of PaiNN models.

    Computes: - Energy: Mean over committee, referenced back to raw DFT scale.
    - Forces: Mean force over committee. - Uncertainty: Standard deviation of
    force vectors across committee.
    """

    def __init__(self, model_dirs, ref_energies, device="cuda:0", cutoff=6.0):
        self.device = torch.device(device)
        self.cutoff = cutoff
        self.ref_energies = {int(k): float(v) for k, v in ref_energies.items()}

        self.models = []
        for p in model_dirs:
            print(f"Loading PaiNN model from {p}")
            m = load_model(str(p))
            m.to(self.device)
            m.eval()
            self.models.append(m)

    def _build_batch(self, atoms):
        pos = torch.tensor(
            atoms.get_positions(), dtype=torch.float32, device=self.device
        )
        z = torch.tensor(
            atoms.get_atomic_numbers(), dtype=torch.long, device=self.device
        )
        nbrs = get_neighbor_list(pos.cpu(), cutoff=self.cutoff, undirected=False)
        nbrs = nbrs.to(self.device)
        offs = torch.zeros((nbrs.shape[0], 3), device=self.device)

        nxyz = torch.cat(
            [z.view(-1, 1).float(), pos], dim=1
        )  # (N, 4) [Z, x, y, z]

        batch = {
            "nxyz": nxyz,
            "nbr_list": nbrs,
            "offsets": offs,
            "num_atoms": torch.tensor([len(atoms)], device=self.device),
        }
        return batch

    def calculate(self, atoms):
        batch = self._build_batch(atoms)

        energies_kcal = []
        forces_kcal_ang = []

        for model in self.models:
            out = model(batch)
            # Energy in kcal/mol
            e = out["energy"].detach().cpu().item()
            # Gradient dE/dx in kcal/(mol*A) -> Force = -grad
            g = out["energy_grad"].detach().cpu().numpy()
            energies_kcal.append(e)
            forces_kcal_ang.append(-g)

        # Convert kcal/mol -> eV (1 kcal/mol = 0.0433641 eV)
        KCAL_TO_EV = 0.04336414
        energies_ev = np.array(energies_kcal) * KCAL_TO_EV
        forces_ev_ang = np.array(forces_kcal_ang) * KCAL_TO_EV  # (M, N, 3)

        # Re-add atomic reference energy E0 to match DFT absolute scale
        e_ref_total = sum(
            self.ref_energies[int(at)] for at in atoms.get_atomic_numbers()
        )
        mean_energy = float(energies_ev.mean()) + e_ref_total
        mean_forces = forces_ev_ang.mean(axis=0)  # (N, 3) in eV/A

        # Compute force uncertainty (std dev per atom across committee in meV/A)
        diff_sq = (forces_ev_ang - mean_forces[None, :, :]) ** 2
        std_per_atom = (
            np.sqrt(np.mean(diff_sq, axis=(0, -1))) * 1000.0
        )  # meV/A, length N
        max_atomic_sd = float(np.max(std_per_atom))  # max over atoms

        results = {
            "energy": mean_energy,
            "forces": mean_forces,
            "max_atomic_sd": max_atomic_sd,
            "std_per_atom": std_per_atom,
        }
        return results


# ==============================================================================
# 3. FHI-AIMS SINGLE POINT DFT RUNNER (VIA SLURM SUBPROCESS)
# ==============================================================================
def run_aims_single_point(atoms, run_dir, trajectory_id, step):
    """Generates FHI-aims inputs, submits via sbatch or srun, and extracts results."""
    calc_dir = run_dir / f"aims_calc_traj{trajectory_id}_step{step}"
    calc_dir.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)

    # 1. Write geometry.in
    write(calc_dir / "geometry.in", atoms, format="aims")

    # 2. Build control.in (copy base settings + append species defaults)
    base_control = CONTROL_IN.read_text()
    species_needed = sorted(set(atoms.get_chemical_symbols()))
    species_text = ""
    for spec in species_needed:
        spec_file = SPECIES_DIR / f"{Path(spec).name if len(spec) > 2 else ''}"
        # Search species default
        for f in SPECIES_DIR.glob(f"*_{spec}"):
            species_text += f.read_text() + "\n"
            break

    full_control = (
        base_control + "\n# --- SPECIES DEFAULTS ---\n" + species_text
    )
    (calc_dir / "control.in").write_text(full_control)

    # 3. Write Slurm job script
    job_script = f"""#!/bin/bash
#SBATCH --job-name=aims_al_pt
#SBATCH --partition=nanotubo
#SBATCH --nodes=1
#SBATCH --ntasks-per-node=32
#SBATCH --time=01:00:00
#SBATCH --output={calc_dir}/aims.out
#SBATCH --error={calc_dir}/aims.err

module purge
module load {AIMS_MODULE}
cd {calc_dir}
mpirun aims.x > aims.out 2>&1
"""
    (calc_dir / "submit.sh").write_text(job_script)

    # 4. Submit and wait for job completion
    print(
        f"[AL] Dispatching FHI-aims calculation for Trajectory {trajectory_id} at step {step}..."
    )
    res = subprocess.run(
        ["sbatch", str(calc_dir / "submit.sh")],
        capture_output=True,
        text=True,
        check=True,
    )
    job_id = res.stdout.strip().split()[-1]
    print(f"[AL] Submitted Slurm Job ID: {job_id}")

    # Poll until job completes
    while True:
        status = subprocess.run(
            ["squeue", "-j", job_id], capture_output=True, text=True
        )
        if job_id not in status.stdout:
            break
        time.sleep(10)

    # 5. Parse output from aims.out
    atoms_dft = read(calc_dir / "aims.out", format="aims-output")
    e_dft = atoms_dft.get_potential_energy()
    f_dft = atoms_dft.get_forces()

    print(
        f"[AL] DFT completed. Energy: {e_dft:.4f} eV, Max Force: {np.max(np.abs(f_dft)):.4f} eV/A"
    )
    return e_dft, f_dft

# 5. ACTIVE LEARNING MAIN LOOP
# ==============================================================================
def main():
    with open(REF_ENERGIES_PATH) as f:
        ref_energies = json.load(f)

    model_dirs = [PAINN_ENSEMBLE_DIR / f"model_{i}" for i in [0, 1, 2]]
    calculator = PainnCommitteeCalculator(
        model_dirs, ref_energies, device=DEVICE, cutoff=CUTOFF
    )

    # Load initial starting structures
    geom_files = list(Path("/home/maria.crist/pax/geometries").glob("*.in"))
    print(f"Loaded {len(geom_files)} initial geometries.")

    trajectories = []
    for i in range(NUM_TRAJECTORIES):
        geom_path = geom_files[i % len(geom_files)]
        atoms = read(geom_path, format="aims")
        trajectories.append(atoms)

    train_xyz_path = WORK_DIR / "al_acquired_train.xyz"
    if not train_xyz_path.exists():
        # Seed with initial baseline
        initial_seed = read("/home/maria.crist/pax/train.xyz", index=":")
        write(train_xyz_path, initial_seed, format="extxyz")

    threshold = UNCERTAINTY_THRESHOLD_INIT
    total_dft_evals = 0

    print("==================================================")
    print("STARTING PAINN ACTIVE LEARNING SIMULATION")
    print(f"Threshold: {threshold:.2f} meV/A | Max DFT Points: {MAX_NEW_POINTS}")
    print("==================================================")

    for step in range(0, MAX_MD_STEPS, SKIP_STEP_MLFF):
        for traj_id, atoms in enumerate(trajectories):

            # 1. Propagate MD for SKIP_STEP_MLFF steps using Committee mean force
            for _ in range(SKIP_STEP_MLFF):
                res = calculator.calculate(atoms)
                f = res["forces"]
                # Simple Velocity Verlet / Langevin integration step
                p = atoms.get_positions()
                atoms.set_positions(
                    p + 0.5 * f * (MD_TIMESTEP_FS * 1e-15) ** 2 / units.fs
                )

            # 2. Check uncertainty at current point
            res = calculator.calculate(atoms)
            uncertainty = res["max_atomic_sd"]  # meV/A

            print(
                f"[Traj {traj_id} | Step {step+SKIP_STEP_MLFF}] Uncertainty: {uncertainty:.2f} meV/A (Thresh: {threshold:.2f})"
            )

            # 3. Trigger Active Learning if uncertainty exceeds threshold
            if uncertainty > threshold:
                print(
                    f"--> [TRIGGER] High uncertainty ({uncertainty:.2f} > {threshold:.2f}) on Trajectory {traj_id}!"
                )

                # Query DFT
                e_dft, f_dft = run_aims_single_point(
                    atoms, WORK_DIR, traj_id, step
                )
                total_dft_evals += 1

                # Save labeled frame to extxyz
                labeled_frame = atoms.copy()
                labeled_frame.info["REF_energy"] = e_dft
                labeled_frame.arrays["REF_forces"] = f_dft
                write(
                    train_xyz_path,
                    labeled_frame,
                    format="extxyz",
                    append=True,
                )

                # Retrain PaiNN models
                retrain_painn_committee(
                    PAINN_ENSEMBLE_DIR,
                    train_xyz_path,
                    ref_energies,
                    epochs=FINE_TUNE_EPOCHS,
                )

                # Reload updated weights
                calculator = PainnCommitteeCalculator(
                    model_dirs, ref_energies, device=DEVICE, cutoff=CUTOFF
                )

                # Dynamically adapt threshold
                threshold = max(15.0, threshold * 1.05)

                if total_dft_evals >= MAX_NEW_POINTS:
                    print(
                        f"[AL] Reached maximum DFT budget ({MAX_NEW_POINTS}). Finishing."
                    )
                    return


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

6.3 KEY PITFALLS & SOLUTIONS
----------------------------

1. Cluster QOS Limits:
   - Your Slurm cluster imposes `QOSMaxSubmitJobPerUserLimit` (typically 1 running
     job + 1 pending job).
   - Ensure you do not submit multi-task array jobs while active learning is
     dispatching DFT single points.

2. Partition Time Limits:
   - Partition `metano` (GPU) allows up to 72 hours (3 days).
   - Partition `etileno` hard-caps at 24 hours.
   - For long active learning explorations with online fine-tuning, ALWAYS use
     `--partition=metano`.
   - For the parallel DFT single points, use `--partition=nanotubo` (32 cores).

3. Atomic Reference Energies (E0):
   - Never feed raw DFT energies into PaiNN training batches. Raw FHI-aims energies
     are centered around -2000 to -5000 eV, whereas PaiNN expects near-zero energy.
   - Always verify that each newly added frame is shifted using `reference_energies.json`.

4. NaN Gradient Guard:
   - Always preserve the `make_safe_loss_fn` and `grad_is_finite` functions from
     `finetune_painn.py`. When an active learning MD step visits an extremely
     distorted, compressed bond geometry, raw network gradients can spike to Inf.
   - The safe loss wrapper cleanly skips the step rather than corrupting model weights.

5. Neighbor List & Cutoff Consistency:
   - The pretrained PaiNN models have a hardcoded radial cutoff of 6.0 Angstroms.
   - Always ensure neighbor lists are constructed with `cutoff = 6.0` and
     `undirected = False` (directed graphs).
